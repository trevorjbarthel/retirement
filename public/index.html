Concurrent Receipt Rules

If your VA disability rating is below 50%, your retirement pay may be reduced by the amount of VA compensation (VA waiver). However, if you qualify for CRDP (Concurrent Retirement and Disability Pay) at 50% or higher VA rating, you can receive both full retirement pay AND full VA compensation with no offset. Members with combat-related disabilities may qualify for CRSC regardless of rating percentage. Consult your finance office for your specific situation.

SBP Premium & Break-Even

Premium = 6.5% of the elected base amount; survivor annuity = 55% of base. Coverage is paid-up only after 360 payments AND age 70. The SBP-DIC offset was repealed in 2023. DFAS SBP →

CRDP vs. CRSC Net Pay

VA comp offsets retired pay dollar-for-dollar. CRDP (auto at 20yr + 50%+) is taxable; CRSC (combat-related, requires DD 2860) is tax-free. Estimates only. DFAS →

Retiree Healthcare Cost (TRICARE Select + FEDVIP)

Terminal Leave vs. Selling Leave Back

Counts against the 60-day career cap.

Selling leave pays base pay only (no BAH/BAS) as a one-time lump sum, taxed at ~22% federal withholding, and is capped at 60 days across your whole career (37 U.S.C. 501). Terminal leave keeps you on active duty — full pay plus tax-exempt BAH/BAS — for the same days, but delays your separation date and any civilian paycheck that would start after it. This compares take-home dollars only; it doesn't weigh starting a civilian job earlier, which sell-back enables and terminal leave doesn't.

What Civilian Salary Do I Need to Break Even?

Military pay looks smaller than it is: BAH and BAS are completely untaxed, and healthcare is nearly free. A civilian offer has to clear a higher bar than your base pay to leave you where you are today. This works out that bar.

Your share of the premium, not the employer's.

Shown separately — deferred pay, not take-home.

Uses 2026 federal brackets and the standard deduction, FICA at 7.65%, and your state's damped effective rate. Your retired pay (if any) is counted as continuing income, which lowers the salary you need. Bonuses, equity, PTO, commute and relocation costs are not modeled — treat the result as the floor of a negotiating range, not a precise target.
